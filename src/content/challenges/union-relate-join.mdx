## Goal

Driftlight's viewing data arrives as two yearly files plus a titles lookup. You combine them the three ways task 1.2 asks about: a **union** for the two years, a **relationship** to the titles, and then a **join** to the same titles, so you see for yourself why a join between tables at different levels of detail can duplicate a measure.

## Get the data

Download the three files into one folder:

<DataFiles />

`sessions_2024.csv` and `sessions_2025.csv` have the same columns, one row per viewing session. `titles.csv` has one row per title, with its `Runtime` in minutes. Read [lesson 1.2](/learn/1.2) first if relationships, joins and unions are new to you.

## Steps

<Step id="union-sessions">
  In Tableau Public, connect to `sessions_2024.csv` (**Text file**). On the Data Source page, double-click the table on the canvas to open its physical layer: "logical tables are like containers for physical tables". Then drag `sessions_2025.csv` from the left pane **directly below** the first table, or double-click **New Union** and drag both files into the **Union** dialog. Tableau adds fields that record where each row came from, such as **Table Name**. Close the physical layer to get back to the logical layer.
</Step>

<Step id="sessions-by-year">
  On a sheet, drag **Start** to Columns (Tableau shows `YEAR(Start)`) and the count measure, named after the table and followed by **(Count)**, to Rows. You should see one bar per year: the union stacked the rows of both files.
</Step>

<Step id="relate-titles">
  Back on the Data Source page (logical layer), drag `titles.csv` onto the canvas. When the "noodle" appears between the two tables, drop it. Tableau tries to match fields itself; in the settings below the canvas, check that the relationship uses **Title ID** on both sides (the fields "must have the same data type").
</Step>

<Step id="runtime-related">
  On a new sheet, drag **Runtime** to Rows and nothing else. Read `SUM(Runtime)` from the tooltip or put Runtime on Label too. Relationships "maintain each table's level of detail", so Tableau adds up the runtimes of the titles table itself, once per title.
</Step>

<Step id="join-titles">
  Now do it the old way. On the Data Source page, take `titles.csv` off the logical canvas. Double-click the sessions table to open the physical layer again, then double-click or drag `titles.csv` onto the join canvas next to the union. Click the join icon (the Venn diagram) and choose **Inner**, with the clause `Title ID = Title ID`.
</Step>

<Step id="runtime-joined">
  Go back to the Runtime sheet. The joined tables are "merged into a single logical table with a fixed combination of data": every session row now carries its title's Runtime, so `SUM(Runtime)` adds each runtime once per session. Compare it with your first number.
</Step>

## Exam angle

- **Union** adds rows (same columns, such as monthly or yearly files), a **join** adds columns across the same row structure, and a **relationship** combines tables at different levels of detail. Relationships are Tableau's default and recommended method.
- A noodle between logical tables is a relationship; a Venn diagram between physical tables, inside a logical table, is a join.
- "My SUM is suddenly far too big after adding a table" points to a join between tables at different levels of detail. The usual fix is a relationship, not a different aggregation.
- The four join types are inner, left, right and full outer. A left join keeps every row of the left table and shows nulls where the right table has no match.
