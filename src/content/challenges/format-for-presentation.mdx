## Goal

Driftlight's board gets two charts: minutes per month split by Movie and Series, and a scatterplot of titles. Your job is to make them readable: colour, shapes, sizes, fonts, animations and legends, all from the Marks card and the Format menu. Along the way you read three numbers from the views.

## Get the data

Download the two files into one folder:

<DataFiles />

`sessions_2025.csv` has one row per viewing session in 2025; `titles.csv` one row per title, with its Type (Movie or Series) and Genre.

## Steps

<Step id="connect">
  Connect to `sessions_2025.csv` with the **Text file** connector. Drag `titles.csv` onto the canvas and drop it on the "noodle" to relate it to the sessions on **Title ID** (lesson [1.2](/learn/1.2)).
</Step>

<Step id="color">
  On Sheet 1, put **Start** on Columns and change it to MONTH (open the pill's menu and pick Month), put **Minutes Watched** on Rows, and drag **Type** to **Color** on the Marks card. A discrete field on Color gets a **categorical palette**, one colour per member, and a colour legend appears. Click **Color > Edit Colors** on the Marks card (or **Edit Colors** in the legend's menu), select Series and give it another colour. Then drag **Minutes Watched** to Color instead, to see a continuous field get a **quantitative** (gradient) legend, and press Undo.
</Step>

<Step id="shapes">
  On a new sheet, put the sessions count measure (named after the table, followed by **(Count)**) on Columns and **Minutes Watched** on Rows, and drag **Title** to **Detail** on the Marks card: one mark per title. With measures innermost on both shelves, Automatic picks the **Shape** mark. Drag **Type** to **Shape**: Tableau gives each member its own shape. Click **Shape** on the Marks card (or **Edit Shape** in the shape legend's menu) and pick a different palette, such as filled shapes.
</Step>

<Step id="size">
  Still on the scatterplot, click **Size** on the Marks card and move the slider: every mark changes, the view doesn't. Then drag **Minutes Watched** to Size: the largest value gets the largest mark, and a size legend appears. Remove it from Size again (drag the pill off the Marks card).
</Step>

<Step id="fonts">
  Choose **Format > Workbook**. The Format Workbook pane replaces the Data pane: pick one font for the whole workbook and a larger size for worksheet titles. Then, on the scatterplot only, choose **Format > Font** and change the header text. Remember the order: workbook first, sheet exceptions after, because a workbook font change overwrites sheet-level font changes.
</Step>

<Step id="animations">
  Choose **Format > Animations**. Under **Workbook Default**, click **On**, choose **Simultaneous** and pick a duration. Back on the scatterplot, drag **Genre** to Filters, untick a few genres and watch the marks move instead of jumping. Try **Sequential** under **Selected Sheet** to override the default for this sheet only.
</Step>

<Step id="legends">
  On the line chart, hide the colour legend, then show it again. The lesson gives no menu path for this, so look for it yourself: start with the legend card's own menu (the same menu that holds **Edit Colors**). When is a legend safe to hide? When the encoding is obvious, for example when each line is labelled.
</Step>

</Callout>

## Exam angle

- **Encoding vs. formatting**: a field dropped on Color, Size or Shape encodes data and adds a legend; clicking Color, Size or Shape without a field restyles every mark, with no legend.
- A discrete field on Color gets a **categorical** palette; a continuous one gets a **sequential or diverging** gradient (Stepped Color, Use Full Color Range).
- Shapes show only with the **Shape** mark type. After ten members the default shapes repeat.
- **Format > Workbook** for every sheet, **Format > Font** for one sheet.
- **Format > Animations**: a workbook default, per-sheet overrides, Simultaneous or Sequential, up to 10 seconds.
