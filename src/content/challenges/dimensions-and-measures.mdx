## Goal

The exam expects you to say what a dimension and a measure usually contain, and what happens when you drag each into a view. In this challenge you connect Tableau Desktop Public Edition (Tableau Public for short) to Driftlight's catalogue of 400 titles, watch Tableau assign the roles, and catch one field it gets wrong.

## Get the data

Download this file:

<DataFiles />

`titles.csv` has one row per film or series: its ID, title, type, genre and sub-genre, release year, rating, runtime in minutes and whether it is a Driftlight original. You also need **Tableau Desktop Public Edition**, free from Tableau Public's website.

## Steps

<Step id="connect-titles">
  Open Tableau Public, choose **Text file** in the Connect pane and open `titles.csv`. Click **Sheet 1**.
</Step>

<Step id="read-data-pane">
  Look at the Data pane. Dimensions sit above a gray line and measures below it. When you connect, Tableau gives each field a role from its values: "a field of numbers is added as a measure", anything else (text, dates, true/false) as a dimension. Before you look closely, write down where you expect Title, Genre, Runtime, Release Year and Original to be. Then compare. Which one is a number that you'd never add up?
</Step>

<Step id="sum-a-year">
  Drag **Release Year** to Rows. Tableau aggregates it, as it does every measure: the pill says `SUM(Release Year)` and the bar adds up the years of all 400 titles. The number is meaningless. Digits don't make a field a measure: years, IDs and postal codes are labels.
</Step>

<Step id="fix-the-role">
  Undo, then drag **Release Year** in the Data pane up across the gray line, among the dimensions. Drag it to Rows again: now you get one header per year, and nothing is added up.
</Step>

<Step id="runtime-by-genre">
  Clear the sheet. Drag **Genre** to Rows and **Runtime** to Columns. The dimension decides how many marks there are (one bar per genre) and the measure decides how big each is (`SUM(Runtime)`). Drag **Type** to Rows as well, to the right of Genre: there are now more bars, and each genre's two bars add up to its bar from before.
</Step>

<Step id="count-titles">
  Clear the sheet. Drag **Type** to Rows and the count measure named after the table (it ends in **(Count)**) to Columns. Every table gets one: it counts the table's rows, here one per title. A dimension can be counted too, as `COUNTD(Title ID)` (Count (Distinct)). Doing so "creates a new temporary measure column": Title ID in the Data pane stays a dimension. Lesson [4.3](/learn/4.3) covers aggregations.
</Step>

<Callout title="Public vs. Desktop">
  <p>Roles, aggregation and the (Count) field work the same in Tableau Public and in Desktop. Tableau Public is a newer version than the exam's 2022.3, so a menu label may differ slightly from the lesson.</p>
</Callout>

## Exam angle

- Dimensions usually hold **qualitative** values (names, categories, dates, places) and slice the view into headers or marks. Measures usually hold **quantitative** values that Tableau aggregates (`SUM` by default).
- Dates are dimensions, even though you can draw them on an axis.
- A number field that's really a label (an ID, a ZIP code, a year) arrives as a measure. The fix is to convert it to a dimension, not to change its aggregation.
- Counting a dimension (`COUNTD(Title ID)`) makes a temporary measure; the field itself stays a dimension.
- Role (dimension or measure) is not the same as continuity (blue or green): that's [task 4.2](/learn/4.2) and the [Blue vs. green simulator](/build/sim/blue-green).
