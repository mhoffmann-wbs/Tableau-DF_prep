## Goal

Blue pills are discrete and draw headers; green pills are continuous and draw an axis. The exam tests this most often with dates, where the same field can be a discrete **date part** or a continuous **date value**. In this challenge you build both from Driftlight's two years of sessions in Tableau Desktop Public Edition (Tableau Public for short) and read the difference off your own views.

## Steps

<Step id="union-years">
  Open Tableau Public, choose **Text file** in the Connect pane and open `sessions_2024.csv`. Then make a **union**: drag `sessions_2025.csv` from the left pane "directly below the first table" on the canvas (or double-click **New Union** and drag both files into the **Union** dialog). A union appends rows, and Tableau adds fields that record where each row came from, such as **Table Name**. Click **Sheet 1**.
</Step>

<Step id="year-headers">
  Drag **Start** to Columns. Tableau picks the level "at which there are multiple instances": with two years of data that's **YEAR**, so you get two blue headers, 2024 and 2025. Drag the count measure (its name ends in **(Count)**) to Rows. Click the **+** on the `YEAR(Start)` pill: Tableau drills down and adds `QUARTER(Start)`, another set of blue headers.
</Step>

<Step id="month-part">
  Remove the QUARTER pill. Open the menu on the `YEAR(Start)` pill and choose **Month** in the **upper** section of the date levels. The pill stays blue and shows twelve headers, January to December. Each header is "the month of May for any year": January 2024 and January 2025 add up in the same bar. This is the view for seasonality.
</Step>

<Step id="month-value">
  Open the pill's menu again and choose **Month** in the **lower**, continuous section. The pill turns green, the headers become an axis, and the marks run through 24 months in order: a date value, "the actual date, truncated to a specific level". This is the view for a trend. Hover over the last point to read December 2025.
</Step>

<Step id="color-legends">
  Drag **Device** to **Color**. A discrete field gets a **categorical palette**: one colour per member and a legend that lists them. Remove it and drag **Minutes Watched** to **Color** instead. A continuous field gets a **quantitative legend**: a gradient. Remove it again.
</Step>

<Step id="discrete-measure">
  In the Data pane, right-click **Minutes Watched** and choose **Convert to Discrete**, then drag it to Rows in place of the count. The pill is blue and draws headers instead of an axis, but it's **still a measure** and still aggregated (`SUM(Minutes Watched)`). Convert it back with **Convert to Continuous**. Now right-click **Device** in the Data pane and look for **Convert to Continuous**: String and Boolean dimensions "can't be continuous", so you can't make Device green.
</Step>

<Callout title="Public vs. Desktop">
  <p>Pills, colours and the date menu work the same in Tableau Public. Tableau Public is a newer version than the exam's 2022.3, so the wording of a menu may differ slightly from the lesson.</p>
</Callout>

## Exam angle

- **Blue = discrete = headers**; **green = continuous = an axis**. On Color, discrete gives a categorical palette and continuous a gradient.
- Blue/green is not dimension/measure: a green date pill is still a dimension, and a blue measure is still aggregated.
- A discrete **date part** (upper section of the date menu) merges the same month of every year; a continuous **date value** (lower section) keeps the years apart on a timeline. "Trend over time" wants the date value; "which month is busiest in any year" wants the date part.
- Only dates, numbers and measures can be continuous. A text field such as Device or Region can't.
- The [Blue vs. green simulator](/build/sim/blue-green) shows the same switches on the Driftlight data.
